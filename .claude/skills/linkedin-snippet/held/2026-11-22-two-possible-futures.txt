The person inside your prospect who likes you has two possible futures, and neither is being promoted for choosing the smaller supplier.

If it goes well, the project went well. That's the outcome everybody expected when they approved the budget.

If it goes badly, they picked an unknown supplier over the name everyone had heard of, and somewhere in the archive is the message where they recommended you.

That asymmetry is the competition. Not your feature list, not your pricing, not the thing you spent two years making genuinely better than the incumbent.

You're competing on who absorbs the career risk of the decision.

And here's the uncomfortable consequence. Your product being better is not neutral here. If choosing you requires your champion to have been clever, you've made their position worse, because being cleverly right earns nothing and being cleverly wrong is unsurvivable.

Being obviously right is safe. Being interestingly right is not.

Which also explains what the large vendor is actually selling, and it's barely about the product. An analyst quadrant your champion can paste into a slide. A reference list the board recognises without explanation. A master agreement procurement has already approved.

None of that is a trick. It's real risk reduction, sold honestly, to a buyer who has correctly identified that their biggest exposure isn't product quality.

So stop trying to look bigger. Looking bigger is a lie your champion has to maintain on your behalf, and they can feel the weight of it.

What's your champion carrying?
