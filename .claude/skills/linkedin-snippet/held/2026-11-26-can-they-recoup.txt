Every owner undercut by a competitor has the same private thought. Surely this is illegal.

There is a test for it, and it is close to unpassable.

In Brooke Group v. Brown and Williamson Tobacco (1993), the Supreme Court set out two prerequisites: "(1) that the prices complained of are below an appropriate measure of its rival's costs and (2) that the competitor had a reasonable prospect of recouping its investment in below-cost prices."

Then the part that should settle it:

"That below-cost pricing may impose painful losses on its target is of no moment to the antitrust laws if competition is not injured: It is axiomatic that the antitrust laws were passed for 'the protection of competition, not competitors.'"

I am not a lawyer and your situation may have features I cannot see. The narrow point is worth accepting quickly: the law is not interested in the fact that this hurts you.

But read that second prerequisite again. It is the best business question available to you.

Can they recoup?

If they cannot, the low price is temporary. They are funding it from somewhere, that somewhere runs out, and waiting is a strategy rather than a loss of nerve. Most small price cutters sit here.

If they can, you are not in a price war. The market has permanently changed shape, and waiting is the one thing guaranteed to lose.

That question is about their cash. Almost every owner I have watched answer it answered a question about their own quality instead, which feels similar and tells you nothing.

Which is it in your market?
