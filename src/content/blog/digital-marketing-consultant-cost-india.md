A founder sends the same one page brief to five people and gets quotes of Rs 12,000 a month and Rs 1.5 lakh a month for what reads like identical work.

Both quotes are honest. That is the part that confuses everybody.

Nobody writes about this properly because every page that ranks for it is published by somebody who sells the service, and putting a number in writing costs them negotiating room. So you get "it depends on your goals" and a contact form.

Here is the version without the hedging. **You are never buying digital marketing. You are buying a number of hours at a level of seniority, and once you know those two things the tenfold range collapses to something sensible.**

## The actual ranges in 2026

Roughly where the Indian market sits, from the rate guides and from the quotes I have read over the last ten years.

A solo freelancer handling one or two channels runs Rs 5,000 to Rs 25,000 a month at the junior end, and Rs 15,000 to Rs 50,000 once they have real experience and a portfolio you can check.

A small agency doing comprehensive work for an SME sits between Rs 20,000 and Rs 75,000 a month. A basic agency retainer with one or two services and monthly reporting is around Rs 50,000. Mid tier, meaning three or four services with a dedicated account manager and fortnightly reporting, is around Rs 1 lakh. Full service with weekly reporting and actual senior strategist time is Rs 2.5 lakh and up.

By service, which is more useful when you are buying one thing rather than everything:

- Local SEO for a small business: Rs 8,000 to Rs 20,000 a month
- SEO for a mid-sized company: Rs 20,000 to Rs 60,000
- Enterprise SEO: Rs 60,000 to Rs 2 lakh and beyond
- A content retainer of four to eight pieces: Rs 15,000 to Rs 60,000
- Email marketing, four to eight campaigns: Rs 8,000 to Rs 25,000
- Paid media management: usually 10% to 20% of ad spend, or a fixed fee

Add 20% to 30% if you are hiring in Mumbai or Bengaluru, which sit meaningfully above the national average. Delhi NCR is a little below those two and still above the rest.

## What actually moves the number

Four things, in order of how much they matter.

**Hours and seniority.** This is the master variable and everything else is downstream. A senior operator in India bills somewhere between Rs 500 and Rs 2,000 an hour domestically. Multiply by the hours your scope genuinely needs and you have your answer before anyone sends a proposal.

**How many channels.** Each additional channel adds tooling, reporting and context switching. Three channels done adequately usually costs more and returns less than one channel done properly, which is the single most common mistake I see in Indian SME marketing budgets.

**Whether ad spend is inside or outside the number.** A Rs 50,000 quote that includes Rs 30,000 of media buy is a Rs 20,000 management fee wearing a disguise. Always ask.

**Where you sit in their client list.** Being somebody's fourth client and being their twelfth are different products at the same price. Ask how many accounts the person on your work is carrying. If nobody will answer, that is the answer.

## Why the Rs 8,000 offer cannot work

You have seen the ads. Complete digital marketing, SEO plus social plus ads plus content, Rs 8,000 a month.

Do the arithmetic from the seller's side rather than yours.

At Rs 500 an hour, which is the floor for anyone competent, Rs 8,000 buys sixteen hours. Take out the monthly report, the two calls, and the tool subscriptions the agency is paying for out of that same amount. You are left with perhaps six to eight hours of actual work.

Now split six hours across four channels, every month.

That is roughly ninety minutes per channel. Ninety minutes is enough to schedule some posts and change a bid. It is not enough to look at a search terms report, read the analytics, form a view and act on it.

So is it a scam?

Mostly not. The person is usually real and usually working. What is fictional is the scope, and it was fictional before anyone signed anything. The dishonesty sits in the list of deliverables, not in the effort.

The reasonable version of a small budget is one channel, done properly, by one person who answers your messages. Rs 15,000 spent on that beats Rs 15,000 spread across four channels every time, and it is not close.

## The GST line nobody puts in the quote

Professional services carry 18% GST in India. A quoted Rs 50,000 is Rs 59,000 leaving your account.

Whether that 18% is a cost or a cash flow item depends entirely on you. If your business is GST registered, you claim it back as input tax credit and the real cost stays Rs 50,000. If you are below the threshold and not registered, it is a genuine 18% increase and you should price it into the decision.

Two sentences that save a lot of confusion later: ask whether the number quoted is inclusive or exclusive of GST, and ask whether the supplier is registered at all. An unregistered supplier cannot give you an invoice you can claim against, which quietly makes their cheaper quote less cheap.

## The uncomfortable bit about domestic rates

Here is the thing that shapes the whole Indian market and rarely gets said out loud.

The same practitioner who bills Rs 500 to Rs 2,000 an hour for an Indian client bills Rs 1,200 to Rs 3,800 an hour for an international one. Same person, same skill, roughly double to triple.

You can see what that does. Anyone good enough to win overseas work drifts towards it, because the hourly is better and the client is often less price sensitive. Which means a domestic buyer looking for senior attention is not really competing with other Indian buyers. They are competing with a company in Texas that has budgeted in dollars.

That is not a complaint about Indian businesses being cheap. It is an explanation of why the good people seem expensive and the affordable people seem thin. The market has two price levels and most buyers only see one of them.

If you want the top end of Indian talent on domestic work, you generally pay closer to the export rate, or you accept a smaller share of their week. Those are the options.

## How to read the next quote you get

Three questions, and the answers tell you more than the number does. How many hours a month is this. Who specifically is doing the work and how many other accounts do they have. What is deliberately not included.

Anybody quoting seriously answers all three without flinching. Anybody who cannot is quoting a package rather than a piece of work, and a package is a price with a story attached.
