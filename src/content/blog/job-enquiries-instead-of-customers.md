The people filling in your contact form are not spam.

They are the most motivated audience your website has. They searched, they found you, they read enough to work out you were a real company, and then they used the only form on the site, because you have not given them anywhere else to go.

So the thing to fix is not your traffic quality. Your website publishes one door and answers two different questions, and the people arriving with the second question are being routed into your sales inbox by your own site structure.

Which means the cheapest fix is a destination, not a filter. That takes about an hour, and most of it is nothing to do with ads.

## First, find out which queries are actually doing this

Search Console, not your analytics. Queries report, last three months.

Filter for queries containing any of these and read what comes back:

careers, career, jobs, job, vacancy, vacancies, hiring, recruitment, salary, internship, intern, apply, openings, freshers, walk-in, naukri.

Then do it again with your own company name plus each of those. That second list is usually the bigger one, and it is the clearest signal in the whole exercise, because somebody typing your company name and the word vacancy has already decided they want to work for you specifically.

Now look at the landing page column next to those queries. You will almost always find they are arriving on your homepage or your about page. Not because Google made a mistake, but because those are the only pages you have that mention your company as an employer at all.

And there is a third category that nothing will fix, so recognise it early. Some trades have a service name that is also a job title. Packers and movers. Catering services. Security services. Housekeeping. Tally operator. If your category is one of those, the query itself is ambiguous and no amount of keyword work resolves it, because the words are genuinely the same words.

## Build the page the other intent is looking for

Here is the part that feels like a distraction from selling and is actually the fix.

Make a careers page. One page, at a sensible URL, linked from your footer.

And it works even if you are not hiring. A page that says, plainly, that there are no openings right now, here is the kind of person who tends to do well here, here is the email address to write to, and we keep applications on file for six months, is an honest and useful page. It will rank for your brand-plus-careers queries almost immediately, because nothing else on the internet is competing for them. And it takes the entire jobs intent off your sales form.

If you are hiring, then use proper markup, and use it correctly. [Google's JobPosting structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/job-posting) is live and fully documented, and it is specifically for real, open roles. The documentation sets out `validThrough` for an expiry date, and says that if the job is filled before that date arrives, you remove the posting. So mark up actual vacancies with real closing dates, and never put markup on a standing "we are always hiring" page, which is the version I see most often and the one that does nothing.

One hour. This is the highest-leverage item in this post and hardly anybody does it, because it does not feel like marketing.

## Route the form by reason, not by length

A word of warning before this one, because it sounds like it contradicts standard advice. I have argued before that you should [cut your form until it hurts](/blog/landing-page-optimisation/), and I stand by that. This is different. This adds a field and still saves you time, because it is about routing rather than friction.

One question, first, above everything else. What is this about?

Three options. I want a quote or to buy something. I am looking for a job. Something else.

Then send each one to a different place. The jobs option goes to an inbox you check weekly with an autoresponder that points at the careers page. The quote option goes wherever your enquiries go now. The third catches suppliers, students, and the person who wants to sell you SEO.

Two practical notes on the Indian version of this. Your enquiries probably arrive on WhatsApp rather than through a form, so the fix moves to your prefilled message text: make it say "Hi, I would like a quote for" rather than just "Hi", because the prefilled text is the only filtering you get on that channel. And if you have a single number on the website doing both sales and HR, the person answering it is doing triage they were never briefed for, which is worth five minutes of conversation with them.

## Before lunch

Three things, in this order.

Open Search Console, filter your queries for the words above, and count. You need the number before you decide how much this is worth.

Then write the careers page. Twenty minutes if you are not hiring, an hour if you are. Link it from the footer and from your about page.

Then add the one routing question to your contact form, and point the jobs branch at the page you just made.

That is the whole job, and the reason it is worth doing this morning is not that the enquiries are annoying. It is that every one of them is somebody who wanted something from you and could not find where to ask, and the same architectural gap is almost certainly costing you enquiries you do want.
