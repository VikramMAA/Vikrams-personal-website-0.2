Two numbers land in the same week and they are the same number.

An agency quotes Rs 25,000 a month for social, SEO and reporting. A reasonable junior performance marketer in Pune or Hyderabad costs about the same in salary.

Founders put those side by side and treat it as a straight choice, which is where the decision goes wrong, because the two are not comparable in any useful way. The first buys a handful of hours a month spread across four disciplines and four other clients. The second buys forty-five hours a week from somebody with nobody to learn from.

Neither of those is a marketing function. They are two different shapes of the same shortfall, and picking between them on price is how you end up a year later with activity and no position.

So the useful question is not which one to buy. It is which knowledge you are willing to let live outside your company.

## The question underneath, which is about forgetting

Here is the sorting rule, and it takes one sentence per item.

**What would it cost you to forget this?**

Some things, if they walked out of the building tomorrow, you would rebuild painfully over two years. Why your buyers hesitate and what the objection actually is. Why your price is what it is and which discount you regret giving. Which channels you have already tried, what you spent, and what happened. The specific words your customers use when they describe the problem.

Other things, if they walked out tomorrow, you would replace with a phone call. The technical SEO audit. The ad account structure. The email automation build. The analytics instrumentation.

That is the whole split, and notice it has nothing to do with who is cheaper.

The continuity side of this argument I have made separately, including why the in-house hire you wanted for stability is the least stable person in the arrangement, in [who your first marketing hire in India should be](/blog/first-marketing-hire-india/). I will not repeat it except to say that it cuts the same way: the institutional memory is the thing to protect, and protecting it is not the same as employing somebody.

## Knowledge that compounds where

The sharper version of the rule, and it explains why the split falls the way it does.

Ask where a given piece of expertise gets better over time.

Your buyer knowledge compounds inside your company. Every call, every lost deal, every awkward question makes it more valuable, and it is worth more to you than to anybody else on earth.

Technical specialism compounds inside the specialist. The person who does paid search account structure all day for eleven clients gets better at it in a way your in-house junior cannot, because they see eleven times the data. Hiring them does not transfer that advantage to you. It removes them from the environment that created it.

So rent the specialism and keep the knowledge. In practice, for most Indian companies under a few crore in revenue, that means one person inside who owns the commercial number and the buyer knowledge, and vendors for the disciplines that need depth and ten hours a month.

## If you rent, buy the reasoning and not just the output

This is the part that decides whether renting costs you anything, and almost nobody puts it in the scope.

A vendor delivers output. Campaigns built, pages published, reports sent. What they keep, usually without anybody intending anything by it, is the reasoning: why that account structure, which keywords were rejected and why, what was tried in March that failed, which audience was dropped after six weeks.

Three years later the relationship ends and you discover that you own a lot of artefacts and none of the decisions. That is how a company with a long agency history still has no institutional memory, and it is the single most expensive thing about renting done badly.

The fix is cheap and you have to ask for it explicitly. Every month, one page: what we changed, what we expected, what happened, and what we decided not to do. Not a dashboard. A decision log, in your drive, in your account.

A good vendor will not mind. A vendor who resists is telling you what the relationship is, and the exit side of that question is worth reading up on, which I did in [choosing an agency by planning for the day it ends](/blog/how-to-choose-a-digital-marketing-agency/).

## The option founders here skip, and the honest version of it

Everybody tells Indian founders to consider a fractional senior. Almost nobody tells them what it costs, so the advice goes nowhere.

The published ranges, and they come overwhelmingly from firms selling fractional services, put monthly retainers somewhere around Rs 1.5 lakh to Rs 3 lakh, with day rates commonly quoted at Rs 25,000 to Rs 50,000 and sometimes higher. Treat those as the sellers' own numbers.

Which means the honest thing to say is that a fractional CMO retainer is not an alternative to your Rs 25,000 agency. It is six times it, and if that is the budget conversation you are in, this advice is for a different company.

But the day rate changes the arithmetic completely, and this is the bit that gets lost.

Two days of a genuinely senior operator, once or twice a year, costs about one month of the agency retainer you are already paying. That is enough to set direction, decide what you are not doing, write the brief your vendors work to, and tell you whether your current spend is defensible. You are buying judgement at the points where judgement changes the outcome, and renting execution for everything else.

That is a structure almost no small Indian company uses and most could afford. The reason they do not is that founders compare a day rate to a monthly salary and recoil, which is comparing a price to a different thing entirely.

## Where I come out

Stop asking who should do the work. Ask what you cannot afford to forget, keep that inside under one person who owns a number, and rent the rest with a decision log attached.

And if your budget is genuinely Rs 25,000 a month, the honest answer is that you are not choosing between an agency and a team. You are choosing which single thing to do properly, and the answer to that is whichever channel produced your last ten customers.

Everything else can wait until the first thing works, which is a less satisfying plan than either quote in front of you and a considerably better one.
