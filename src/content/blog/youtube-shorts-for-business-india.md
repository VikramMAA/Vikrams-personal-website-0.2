The Reel you posted in October is gone. Not deleted, just unreachable, somewhere in a grid nobody scrolls back through.

The Short you posted the same week is still sitting at its own URL, with a title and a description, answering a question somebody is going to type in March.

That is the only difference that matters for a service business, and it is not about reach.

So the answer to whether Shorts are worth it depends on what you think you are making. If you are making content, Reels are easier and the audience is already there. If you are making findable answers that happen to be video, a Short has an address and a Reel does not, and that changes what you shoot, how you title it and how long you expect it to keep working.

## Why the address is the whole argument

Here is the mechanical version, and I want to be careful about what is documented and what is inference.

Google's guidance on video in Search is explicit that a video is eligible for video features only if its watch page is indexed. It also asks for a unique title and description for the video on that page, a valid thumbnail at a stable URL, and a page where watching the video is the main purpose rather than supporting content.

A YouTube Short meets all four of those structurally. It sits on a watch page whose entire purpose is that video, YouTube supplies a stable thumbnail, and you control the title and the description. A Reel has no watch page of that kind for Google to index at all.

Now the hedges, because they matter. Google's documentation does not single out short vertical video or say it is treated any differently from a regular video. And it states plainly that an indexed watch page does not mean the video itself will be indexed.

So this is a precondition rather than a promise. You are not guaranteed anything. But a precondition you can satisfy beats one that is structurally unavailable, and that is the honest case for choosing Shorts over Reels when discoverability is the point.

## The WhatsApp test

Here is the part that is specific to selling in India, and I think it settles the format question on its own.

Your recommendations travel by forward. Somebody's cousin asks a question in a family group, and your customer pastes a link.

A YouTube link pastes into WhatsApp and plays for anybody who taps it. No app, no account, no sign in. The uncle who has never installed Instagram watches it on the first tap.

A Reel link asks that person to install something or log in. Most of them do not, and the forward dies there.

**In a market where the referral arrives as a forwarded link, the format that opens for a stranger wins.**

## Which businesses should actually bother

Being honest about this, because video is sold as universal and it is not.

It is worth it when the buying decision contains a "how does this actually work" or "is this safe" question. Interior designers, equipment dealers, chartered accountants explaining a filing, anybody whose customer is nervous and currently resolving that nervousness by watching somebody else's video.

It is not worth it when the purchase is habitual or decided on price. Nobody watches a video before buying atta. A category that is quiet stays quiet on video, and shooting forty Shorts will not create a question that buyers do not have.

If you are a clinic, read [what Indian medical conduct rules rule out](/blog/local-seo-for-clinics-india/) before you shoot anything, because the standard advice on this topic breaks those rules comfortably.

## The format, and two traps

One question per video. Answer it in the first five seconds, then explain why. That order is the opposite of how most people shoot, and it is what keeps somebody watching who arrived from search rather than from a feed.

Shoot on a phone, vertical, in whatever room you actually work in. Produced video performs worse here than it does in a brand campaign, because the thing being judged is whether you know the answer.

Forty five to ninety seconds for most answers. You can run to three minutes, since the cap went up from sixty seconds in October 2024, and the only good reason to use the extra time is that the answer genuinely needs it.

Two traps worth knowing before you upload.

A horizontal video publishes as a regular video rather than a Short, whatever its length. If you shot it landscape out of habit, you have made something else.

And there is no fifteen second minimum. That belief comes from the default timer in the app's recording screen, not from any rule, and it makes people pad answers that were finished at eleven seconds.

## Titles and descriptions, where this is won

This is the step that separates a Short that works for a year from one that works for a day, and it is the step everybody replaces with hashtags.

The title is the question in the words a person actually types. Not a hook. "How much does modular kitchen work cost per square foot" rather than "The kitchen mistake costing you lakhs". The second one performs better in a feed and the first one is still being found next winter.

The description is three or four sentences that answer the question in text, because text is what gets read by things that cannot watch. Then one link to the page on your site that covers the topic properly.

That is it. No block of twenty hashtags, no "link in bio", no paragraph about your company.

## One shoot, three places

Shoot once, vertically, and then stop treating the three destinations as the same job.

The Short gets the searchable title and the written description, because it is going to be found later.

The Reel gets a different opening line, because the feed decides in the first couple of seconds whether it travels at all, and that is a genuinely different craft. I have written about [what actually makes a post travel on Instagram](/blog/instagram-growth-without-ads/) and will not repeat it here beyond saying that the thing which works there is not the thing which works in search.

And the Short gets embedded on the page of your own site that answers the same question, where it does double duty for a reader who prefers watching.

The one rule: do not paste the identical caption into all three. It is the clearest signal that nobody thought about any of them.

## Before lunch

Twenty minutes and your phone.

Write down the five questions you answer most often on calls. Actual sentences, in the words customers use, not topics.

Take the first one, hold the phone vertically, and answer it in under ninety seconds starting with the answer.

Then upload it with that question as the title, write four sentences in the description, and link to the relevant page on your site. The whole thing is doable before lunch and you will know by next month whether the category rewards it.
