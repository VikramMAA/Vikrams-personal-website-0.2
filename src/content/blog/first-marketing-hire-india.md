The standard reason to hire marketing in-house rather than buy it is continuity. Somebody who learns your business, accumulates context, and is still there in year three.

In India that is the weakest argument available, and it is the one almost every founder reaches for.

Because the thing you can buy here, more cheaply and more deeply than almost anywhere, is execution. Meta ads operators, SEO people, video editors, writers, all available by the month at prices that make a salary look extravagant. What you cannot buy is the person who decides what any of them should be doing and carries the consequence when it does not work.

So the answer is: hire the decision, or do not hire yet. Those are the two defensible positions, and the common first hire sits between them and achieves neither.

## What you are actually buying at each price

Run the arithmetic before the job description, because the comparison is not close to what founders assume.

A digital marketing manager with a few years behind them costs somewhere in the region of Rs 6 to 13 lakh a year. Call it Rs 75,000 a month, fully loaded and ignoring the cost of managing them.

That range is wide because the published figures are a mess, and it is worth knowing why before you anchor on one. Indeed's average sits near Rs 3.14 lakh off 404 self-reported salaries. Glassdoor lands around Rs 7.5 lakh. AmbitionBox data puts mid-level managers at Rs 6.8 to 10.2 lakh. One profile-based site reports a median of Rs 17 lakh. These are not measuring the same job: "manager" covers a two-years-experience campaign runner and a person managing four people, and most of the samples are self-reported. Take the band, not anybody's single number.

For that same Rs 75,000 you could have a mid tier agency handling three or four channels, or two or three decent freelance specialists, or one genuinely senior freelance operator for ten hours a month. I went through [what consultants and agencies actually charge here](/blog/digital-marketing-consultant-cost-india/) in more detail, and the shape of it has not changed.

Notice what the employee is not buying you. Not more hours. Not better channel skill, because the freelancer who does nothing but Google Ads all week is better at Google Ads than your generalist will ever be.

**The employee is only worth the premium if what you are buying is judgement and ownership, because you can buy every other input cheaper.**

That is not an argument against hiring. It is an argument about what to hire for, and it rules out most of the job ads I see.

## The continuity argument runs backwards

Here is where the usual logic inverts, and it is worth being careful with the evidence.

Aon's survey of more than 1,200 organisations across 45 industries puts Indian attrition at **18%** for 2026-27, up from 16.2% the year before, which ends two years of decline. They attribute the rise to competition for specialist skills and to the spread of AI tools.

Two honest caveats. That is an economy-wide figure across all functions, not a marketing number, and I could not find a credible marketing-specific tenure figure anywhere. Other projections for the same period came in nearer 16.5%, so the sources do not agree. What I would take from it is the direction and the order of magnitude rather than the decimal.

Now set that against a junior marketing career in an Indian metro. A good two-years-experience performance marketer in Bengaluru or Gurgaon is the most mobile employee you will ever hire. The market bids for exactly that profile, the jump is usually worth thirty or forty percent, and nobody thinks badly of them for taking it.

So the person you hired for continuity is the one least likely to provide it. Meanwhile the agency you worried about, with the account manager you do not love, has been on your business three years and still has the old campaign structures.

I am not claiming vendors are more loyal. They are just differently fragile: an agency loses one person off your account and the firm absorbs it, where your in-house hire leaving takes the entire institutional memory out of the building in one afternoon.

## The hire that feels safe and does nothing

The most common first marketing hire in India is a Digital Marketing Executive, one to three years of experience, brought in to coordinate the agencies and run the day to day.

It is the worst available option and it is popular because it feels prudent.

Think about what that person can actually do. They cannot fire the agency, because that is the founder's call. They cannot brief it properly, because briefing requires knowing what the business needs to prove this quarter, and nobody has given them that. They cannot overrule a vendor's recommendation, because they have less channel experience than the vendor.

What they can do is forward emails, collect reports and sit in reviews. So a coordination layer appears between the founder and the work, the founder stops attending the agency calls because somebody is handling it, and the quality of decisions drops while the volume of activity stays constant.

Then it looks like a marketing problem. The agency gets blamed, or replaced, and the new agency inherits the same absence of a brief.

**You cannot delegate a decision to somebody who is not allowed to make it.**

## What the role should own instead

If you are hiring, hire for the shortest job description you will ever write.

One person who owns the number. Not channels, not deliverables, the actual commercial number the business needs marketing to move this year. They decide what gets tried, they brief and they sack the vendors, and they come to you with a position rather than a status update.

Three consequences worth stating plainly.

It is a more senior and more expensive hire than the one you were planning, and you will hire one instead of two. Fine. Two people executing without a decision maker is the situation you are trying to escape.

The title matters more than it should, because it determines who applies. "Marketing Lead" or "Head of Growth" brings in people who expect to own an outcome. "Digital Marketing Executive" brings in people who expect to be told, and you will then be annoyed that they wait to be told.

And you have to actually hand over the authority, including the authority to stop things you started. A founder who hires an owner and keeps the decisions has bought an expensive coordinator.

## The honest case for not hiring yet

This is the option nobody presents and it is often right.

If your last ten customers all came from one channel, and you understand why, you do not have a marketing management problem. You have an execution problem, and execution is the cheap thing here. Buy the specialist, keep the decision yourself, and spend the saved salary on actually doing more of what works.

The founder should hold marketing judgement for longer in India than in most markets, for the straightforward reason that the supporting cast is so purchasable. The moment to hire is when you are the constraint: when you know what should happen and have no hours in which to decide it.

That is a real moment and you will recognise it. It is not the same as being tired of agency calls.

## Where I come out

Look at your draft job description and find the word that describes what the person owns. If the only candidates are "campaigns", "channels" or "coordination", you are about to spend a salary on a layer rather than a decision.

Either rewrite it around the number and pay what that person costs, or close the role and hire two specialists for half of it.

The thing to avoid is the middle, which feels like progress and is the most expensive way to stay exactly where you are.
