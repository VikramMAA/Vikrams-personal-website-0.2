The agency is twelve miles away. At four in the afternoon that is fifty minutes.

So the in-person meeting you are paying a premium for is a thing that happens once a quarter, badly, with somebody arriving apologetic about the 405. Everybody knows this. Nobody says it during the pitch.

Here is the fact that should settle the argument before it starts. Los Angeles County contains 88 incorporated cities. An agency in Santa Monica and an agency in Pomona are both local, and they are describing two completely different places with different incomes, different languages at home and different commutes.

Which means the word local, here, is a claim about a freeway rather than a claim about your market.

That is not the whole answer though, because there are two things a genuinely local partner does buy you, and neither of them is the meeting.

## What people actually mean when they say they want local

Be fair to the instinct, because it is not stupid.

An owner asking for a local agency is almost never asking for geography. They are asking for three things: somebody who will answer the phone, somebody who cannot disappear, and somebody they can look in the eye if the thing goes wrong.

Geography feels like a proxy for all three. It is a bad one. A firm eight miles away can ghost you just as easily, and the published view on this is consistent: what predicts a good engagement is communication quality rather than communication format, and relevant category experience rather than a shared area code.

The accountability you want comes from a contract, a named person, a weekly rhythm and clear reporting. If you cannot get those, proximity will not save you. If you can get them, distance stops mattering.

I went through [the model question separately](/blog/agency-vs-freelancer-vs-in-house/), agency against freelancer against in house, and that decision matters considerably more than the map does.

## The first real exception, and the version of it people get wrong

The Hispanic and Latino market in Los Angeles is the strongest argument for local knowledge, and most people state it incorrectly.

The lazy version is that you need Spanish creative or you are leaving money on the table. Check the numbers. Around 20% of LA County residents aged five and over speak Spanish at home, and roughly 85% of those speak English very well.

So this is not primarily a translation problem. A campaign in English reaches most of these customers perfectly well.

What it is, is a cultural register problem, and the geography makes it concrete: of the 88 cities in the county, 35 are 50% or more Hispanic or Latino. Household composition, who makes the purchase decision, which holidays and moments matter, what a trustworthy business looks like, which channels get used. Those differ, and they differ by city.

The failure mode I would watch for is creative that was written in English for a general audience and then run through translation, which produces Spanish that is grammatically fine and reads like a form. If you are going to work in Spanish, it has to be written in Spanish by somebody who lives in that register.

Can a remote agency do this? Yes, if it has actually done it, which is a portfolio question rather than an address question. The thing to ask is not are you in LA, it is show me work you wrote in Spanish and tell me who wrote it.

## The second real exception, which is a data skill

Knowing which of the 88 cities your buyers live in.

This is where out-of-market agencies genuinely lose money, and it shows up as a radius. Somebody sets a fifteen mile circle around your address because that looks reasonable on a map, and in this county a fifteen mile circle contains four or five distinct markets, two of which cannot afford you and one of which will never cross that stretch of freeway to reach you.

The right setup is usually not a radius at all. It is a specific list of cities or ZIP codes drawn from where your existing customers actually came from, which is a question you can answer today from your own records.

That is the test I would apply to any agency, local or not. Ask how they would decide your geographic targeting. If the answer involves a radius and no reference to your customer list, they have never thought about this county properly, and sitting in Culver City did not help them.

Multi-site businesses have the same problem one level up, which is [why one location outperforms the others](/blog/multi-location-local-seo/) more often than the ad account explains.

## What travels perfectly well over a video call

Almost everything else, and it is worth saying plainly so the exceptions above do not get inflated.

Search and paid media. Analytics and measurement. Email and lifecycle work. Technical SEO. Content. Conversion work on your site. DTC creative production, which in this city is frequently the reason a company hires locally and is also the thing most easily done by whoever is best at it, wherever they are.

None of that is improved by the person being able to drive to you. Some of it is actively better remote, because a remote firm hired from a national pool rather than from whoever was willing to commute to Century City.

The one genuine physical requirement is shooting. If your plan needs photography or video at your premises, somebody has to be in the room. That is a day of work you can hire locally on its own, and it is a strange reason to choose a year-long strategic partner.

## The questions that actually test an agency

None of these are about location, and all of them are harder to answer than where is your office.

Who specifically will do the work, and what else are they on this month.

Show me a company like mine that you took from where I am to where I want to be, and tell me what went wrong in the middle.

How would you decide my geographic targeting, and what would you need from me to do it.

What would make you tell me to stop spending.

How do I leave, and what do I keep.

An agency down the street that fumbles those is worse than one in Denver that answers them cleanly, and [the cost conversation](/blog/how-much-does-a-digital-marketing-agency-cost/) is a separate matter that proximity has never made cheaper.

## Where I come out

In a county of 88 cities, local is a weak signal dressed up as a strong one. Buy category experience, buy communication discipline, and buy somebody who asks about your customer list before they set a radius.

Hire local specifically when you need Spanish-language creative written rather than translated, when you need somebody in the room with a camera, or when a person's genuine knowledge of a specific city is doing real work in your plan.

Otherwise the question is not where they sit. It is whether they will still be answering the phone in month seven.
