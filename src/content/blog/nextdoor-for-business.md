If you have been posting about your own business from your personal Nextdoor account, stop today.

That stopped being allowed in 2026. Nextdoor changed its self-promotion policy so that promoting a business, a service or a paid opportunity from a neighbor account is a violation, whether it's a post, a comment in the main feed, or an unsolicited direct message. The platform says that content can be automatically suppressed or blocked, that anything slipping through can still be reported and removed, and that repeat attempts to work around it can restrict your account access.

A lot of trades and solo operators were doing exactly this, because it worked for years.

The replacement is free, which is the part worth your next twenty minutes. Claim the Business Page, get the recommendations moving, and treat the ads as a later decision rather than the first one. For most local service categories the free presence is worth more than the paid product, and the reason has nothing to do with price.

## Why the free page outranks the ad budget here

Recommendations on Nextdoor sit inside an address-verified neighborhood. Not a metro, not a city, a neighborhood.

That is the one structural thing this platform has that Google and Yelp don't, and almost nobody uses it deliberately.

Think about what decides profit on a trades job. It isn't the ticket, it's drive time. Three jobs inside one subdivision beats five jobs scattered across forty minutes of highway, every day of the week. So the question for your social proof isn't how much of it you have, it's where it is.

**Twelve recommendations concentrated in the three neighborhoods you actually want to serve will do more for your margin than two hundred reviews spread across the whole metro.**

Nextdoor is the only local platform where you can build proof that is indexed to the same geographic unit your routing is. Use it for density, not volume.

## You're allowed to ask, which is not true everywhere

This is where people get it backwards, usually because they learned review etiquette on a platform with the opposite rule. Nextdoor lets you ask a customer for a recommendation. Yelp does not, and I've written about [why that difference matters more than the platforms themselves](/blog/yelp-vs-google-business-profile/).

Your Business Page has a reputation area with a "get recommendations" option that generates the link to send. The whole ask is one sentence after a completed job.

"If you're on Nextdoor, a recommendation would help me out when you get a minute."

No incentive, no suggested wording, no second follow up. The incentive part is not optional etiquette, it's the FTC's rule on reviews and testimonials, which prohibits paying for or rewarding reviews, alongside Nextdoor's own guidelines. And if the person recommending you is your cousin or your employee, the material connection has to be disclosed.

Nextdoor's own business resources put some numbers on what a complete page does. Pages with recommendations are claimed to get up to 19 times the page views, a logo 7 times, a photo gallery 2.6 times, and businesses with five or more recommendations see about 30% higher engagement on their posts. Those are the platform's figures about its own product, so discount them accordingly. The direction is the useful part, and the direction says a page with a logo, photos and a handful of recommendations is a different object from an empty one.

## Judging the ads honestly

Self-serve is cheap. You can start at roughly $3 to $5 a day on the boost option or around $10 a day through the ads center, with recommended minimum bids near $2 CPC and $10 CPM. Reported averages land somewhere around $2.50 to $3.50 a click and about $20 per thousand impressions. Neighborhood sponsorships run near $150 a month in sought-after zip codes.

For contrast, the sponsored post product aimed at national brands carries a minimum in the region of $25,000 a month. That gap tells you who the paid system was designed around.

Now the part that matters more than the rate card. A click costs $3 instead of $40 because the person was scrolling a neighborhood feed, not typing "emergency plumber open now" at eleven at night. Cheap clicks and urgent clicks are different products, and a lot of home services operators judge Nextdoor ads against a search benchmark and conclude the platform is broken when the real issue is that they bought discovery and measured it like demand capture.

So run ads here for things that work on discovery timing:

- A new location or a service area you just expanded into
- Seasonal pre-booking, gutters before the leaves, AC tune-ups before the first hot week
- A category people don't search for until they see it, like crawl space work or exterior lighting

Don't run them to catch emergency demand. That isn't where this audience is.

## The etiquette that actually gets you removed

Four things, and the first one is the new one.

Promoting from your personal account, now a straight violation. Posting your business into general neighborhood threads instead of from your page. Offering anything in exchange for a recommendation. And unsolicited DMs, which were always unpopular and are now explicitly named.

The "can anyone recommend a good electrician" threads are the most valuable real estate on the platform and also where the method changed this year. Before you reply to one, check what your Business Page is permitted to do in those threads under the current policy, because the answer moved in 2026 and the old habit is the thing being enforced against.

## One timing note worth having

The 2026 Fave Awards closed on 30 September. Voting ran from 4 September, winners are picked by neighborhood, city and business subcategory, three per category, and the prize is a trophy badge on the page plus a spot in the discovery hub.

Which means if you are reading this now, that window has gone for the year. Put the first week of September in next year's calendar and build recommendations in the meantime, because the awards are a four week event and the recommendations are the asset that works the other forty eight.

## Before lunch

An hour, in this order.

Search your business name on Nextdoor before you create anything. A page may already exist, built from third party data or created when a neighbor recommended you, and it may already have recommendations sitting on it. Claim that one rather than starting a duplicate.

Add the logo and at least six photos of finished work, not stock images.

Then pick your last twenty customers, and send the recommendation link only to the ones in the neighborhoods you want more work in. That selection is the whole point.
