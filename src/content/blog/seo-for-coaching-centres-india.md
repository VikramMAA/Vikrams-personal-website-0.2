Picture the hero section on almost every coaching centre website in the country. A big number, 500+ SELECTIONS, and underneath it in small grey type, a disclaimer nobody can read on a phone.

That layout is the problem, and not for the reason you think.

The CCPA's guidelines for the coaching sector require a disclaimer to be displayed prominently and in the same font as the claim it qualifies. Same font. So the fix is not a better worded disclaimer. It is a redesign of the section, because a disclaimer set at the same size as a 48 pixel headline is a different piece of design entirely.

Start there, because it is the single most common compliance gap on coaching websites and it takes an afternoon.

Then the bigger point, which is where the actual admissions come from. You can still publish results, with specific disclosures attached. But the compliant version of a results claim is weaker as marketing than the version you have been running, and that pushes your search strategy somewhere better anyway: the questions parents ask before they ever think about your rank list.

## What the guidelines actually require

The Guidelines for Prevention of Misleading Advertisement in the Coaching Sector were issued on 13 November 2024 and apply, per the legal commentary I have read, to centres supporting more than fifty students. They cover all your advertising, which includes your website, your Instagram and the reel your front desk filmed last week.

The parts that matter most for a website:

No false claims about the number of students selected, their ranks, or success rates. No "100% selection", no guaranteed result, no guaranteed job.

Where a successful candidate's photograph appears, the advertisement must disclose details including the rank secured and how long that person was enrolled with you.

Prior written consent before you use any candidate's name, photograph, testimonial or video.

Testimonials must be representative rather than selectively chosen.

Disclaimers displayed prominently, in the same font as the claim.

And endorsers carry their own liability under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, so the local influencer you paid to recommend the centre is expected to have verified the claims themselves.

Enforcement is real rather than theoretical. Taxmann's analysis reports penalties on 24 coaching centres totalling about Rs 77.6 lakh.

I am not your compliance advisor and this is not legal advice. I have read the commentary rather than the notified text, and anybody running a centre should put their advertising in front of somebody who has read the text properly. The cost of being wrong here is not a ranking drop.

## The part that will annoy you, and why it helps

Once you put the enrolment duration next to a rank, some of your results stop being flattering.

The student who joined in January and cleared in May was mostly somebody else's work, and the disclosure says so. That is precisely what the rule is for.

But read what it does to your proof. **Your genuinely long-term students become your strongest evidence, and you are now required to say so.** A rank with "enrolled for 26 months" beside it is a claim about your teaching. A rank with "enrolled for 4 months" is a claim about the student.

So the results block worth building, with written consent on file for each entry, carries four things per student. Name, exam and year, rank secured, duration enrolled. Set in the same type size as whatever headline sits above it.

Fewer students, longer durations, honest numbers. It will convert better than the wall of 500 faces, because a parent comparing three centres can finally tell them apart.

## What parents actually search, which is not your results

Here is where the compliance constraint and the search opportunity happen to point in the same direction.

Parents and students do search for the obvious thing, exam plus locality. JEE coaching in Kothrud, NEET classes in Kukatpally. Those queries are competitive, every centre in the area is chasing them, and the map pack decides most of it.

What almost nobody answers are the decision questions, and there are far more of them.

Whether to start in class 9 or wait until 11. Whether online coaching genuinely works for a student who needs supervision. What batch size actually changes about outcomes. How to judge a faculty member before paying. Whether to switch centres mid year after a bad test series. What happens to the fee if a student drops out in month three. How much of the day a serious preparation schedule really takes, so a parent can decide whether to also keep the school tuition going.

Not one of those involves a results claim. So not one of them carries compliance exposure, and almost every centre is ignoring them to publish rank lists instead.

**Write about the decision, not the outcome.** The rule pushes you there and the search volume is there anyway.

## The one page I would build first

A fee page with actual numbers on it.

Nobody in this sector publishes fees. Everybody searching wants them, and the search behaviour shows it: the phrase gets appended to centre names constantly, and the pages that rank are forum threads and aggregators guessing.

Put the real structure on a page. What the course fee covers. What the test series costs if it is separate. Instalment options and dates. Material costs. What happens on withdrawal, in month one and in month six. Whether there is a scholarship route and what it actually requires.

You will lose a few enquiries from people who were never going to afford it, which is a saving rather than a loss, and you will gain the ones who were comparing you against a centre that made them call to find out.

This is also the page that survives the compliance question completely, because a fee is a fact.

## The admission window, which is a calendar problem

The enquiry volume arrives in a short burst after board and entrance results, and most centres staff for the phones and forget the timing of the content.

Here is the part people get backwards. Content published during the admission window does not rank during the admission window. A new page needs weeks to get crawled, assessed and to accumulate any signals at all.

So the decision pages above have to be live well before the rush, not written in response to it. If your window opens in May, those pages belong on the site by February. The work you do in the window itself is answering enquiries, not publishing.

That is the whole scheduling insight and it is worth more than any individual tactic on this page.

## Before lunch

Three things, about ninety minutes.

Open your homepage on a phone and look at the hero claim and its disclaimer. If the disclaimer is smaller than the claim, that is your first fix and it is a design change.

Then open your results section and check, for each student shown, whether you hold written consent and whether the rank and the enrolment duration are both stated. Make a list of the entries that fail.

Then write down the five questions parents actually ask you on the phone before they enquire about fees. Those five are your next five pages, and they are the ones nobody in your city has written.
