## How much does a digital marketing agency cost per month?

Between **$1,500 and $10,000 a month** for most small and mid-sized businesses in the US, and between **Rs 20,000 and Rs 4 lakh** in India. Single-channel work for a small business sits at the bottom of that band; multi-channel work with real strategy and senior time attached sits at the top. Below $1,000 a month, or Rs 15,000 in India, you are buying scheduling and a monthly report rather than marketing.

That is the band where the actual work happens. You will find quotes below and above it. Above $25,000 you are in enterprise territory with a team assigned to you.

Broken down by where you sit:

| Your situation | US monthly fee | India monthly fee |
|---|---|---|
| Single channel, small business | $1,000 – $3,000 | Rs 20,000 – Rs 50,000 |
| Multi-channel, small business | $3,500 – $5,000 | Rs 50,000 – Rs 1.5 lakh |
| Mid-market, 51 to 250 employees | $4,000 – $12,000 | Rs 1.5 lakh – Rs 4 lakh |
| Enterprise | $25,000+ | Rs 6 lakh+ |

Now the more useful part, because the number on its own tells you very little.

## Translate the price into hours, because that is what you are buying

Agency hourly rates in the US run roughly $100 to $300, varying by discipline. SEO tends to sit at $75 to $150. Paid search is $100 to $175. Social media lands around $90 to $200. In India the equivalent figure is Rs 500 to Rs 2,000 an hour for a senior operator working domestically.

So take a $3,000 monthly retainer and divide it by a blended $150 an hour.

Twenty hours. That is your month.

Now ask how many channels that twenty hours is being spread across, and how much of it goes to the monthly report and the two status calls. If the answer is four channels, you are buying about four hours per channel per month, and four hours is enough to keep something ticking over but not enough to improve it.

**The question that makes a quote legible is not "how much" but "how many hours, and whose."** A $5,000 retainer where a senior person spends fifteen hours is a completely different product from a $5,000 retainer where a junior spends thirty five, and both are sold with the same slide deck.

Ask directly. Anyone quoting seriously will tell you.

## Agency fees by engagement type

"Pricing" and "fees" get asked as separate questions, and they deserve separate answers, because the monthly retainer is not the only thing on the menu and it is frequently the wrong thing to buy first.

| Engagement | What it is | US | India |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Monthly retainer** | Ongoing execution across one or more channels | $1,500 – $12,000 / mo | Rs 20,000 – Rs 4 lakh / mo |
| **Project** | Fixed scope with an end date: site build, migration, campaign launch | $3,000 – $30,000 | Rs 75,000 – Rs 8 lakh |
| **Audit** | Diagnosis and a written roadmap, no execution | $1,500 – $7,500 | Rs 25,000 – Rs 1.5 lakh |
| **Fractional senior** | A senior operator for a defined slice of their week | $3,000 – $10,000 / mo | Rs 1 lakh – Rs 3 lakh / mo, or Rs 15,000 – Rs 40,000 a day |

If you have never worked with the agency before, the audit line is almost always the right first purchase. It costs a fraction of a year's retainer and it tells you two things at once: what is actually wrong, and whether these people are any good. A retainer signed before a diagnosis is a bet placed before the cards are dealt.

The Indian figures deserve their own note, because the domestic market has two price levels and most buyers only ever see one. I have written that up separately in [what digital marketing actually costs in India](/blog/digital-marketing-consultant-cost-india/), including the 18% GST line that almost never appears in the quoted number and the reason the genuinely good people look expensive.

## The three fee models, and which one to push for

**Monthly retainer.** The dominant model by a distance, and roughly three quarters of agencies now use it as their primary structure. Predictable for both sides, which is why it won. Ask what happens to unused hours and whether they roll over. Usually they do not.

**Project-based.** A fixed price for a defined piece of work. A website build, a migration, an audit. Good for one-off work and for testing whether you like working with someone before committing to twelve months.

**Percentage of ad spend.** Typically 10% to 20%, most often 15% to 20%. Spend $10,000 on ads and the fee is $1,500 to $2,000.

This last one is worth a moment. The structural problem is obvious once you look at it: the agency earns more when you spend more, whether or not the extra spend produced anything, and getting the same result on a smaller budget reduces their fee. Plenty of good agencies moved to flat retainers specifically to remove that conflict. If you are offered percentage of spend, ask for a flat management fee instead and see what happens.

## Compared to what? The in-house numbers

Most people asking this question are really asking whether to hire someone instead, so here are those numbers.

The fully loaded cost of an employee runs about 1.3 to 1.5 times base salary once you add payroll taxes, benefits, equipment, software and the management time. A $100,000 marketer costs you $130,000 to $150,000.

A complete in-house team, meaning a marketing manager, a content writer, a designer and a paid media specialist, starts around $250,000 to $350,000 a year in total compensation, plus another $15,000 to $30,000 in tools and licenses.

Against that, an agency at $3,000 to $15,000 a month is $36,000 to $180,000 a year.

That comparison gets used as an argument for agencies and it is only half honest, so here is the other half. The in-house person is yours full time and accumulates context about your business that an agency carrying eight other accounts never will. The agency gives you breadth, specialist tools already paid for, and someone who has seen your problem at forty other companies.

The genuinely useful framing is that they solve different problems. One senior in-house generalist plus one specialist agency for the hard channel beats either alone for most companies between $2m and $20m in revenue. If that is the live question for you, [agency vs freelancer vs in house](/blog/agency-vs-freelancer-vs-in-house/) works through it properly.

## What actually moves the number

Four things, and they are not equally important. Roughly in order of impact, with the direction and rough size of the effect:

| Variable | Direction | Rough magnitude |
|---|---|---|
| **Seniority and hours** | More senior, more hours, higher fee | Dominates everything else. Doubling seniority roughly doubles the rate for the same hours |
| **Number of channels** | Each channel added raises the fee | +20% to +40% per channel, and it dilutes the hours already committed |
| **Ad spend inside or outside the fee** | Inside the fee makes the quote look bigger and the service smaller | Can account for 50%+ of a headline number |
| **Your rank in their client list** | Being client fourteen costs the same as being client four, and buys less | No fee effect, large delivery effect |

**Seniority and hours** dominates everything else, which is why it is worth asking about before anything on the deliverables list.

**Number of channels.** Every added channel brings its own tooling, reporting and context switching. Three channels done adequately usually costs more and returns less than one done properly.

**Whether ad spend sits inside or outside the fee.** A $5,000 quote that includes $3,000 of media is a $2,000 management fee in disguise. Always ask which it is.

**Your position in their client list.** Being someone's fourth client and their fourteenth are different products at identical prices. Ask how many accounts the person actually working on yours is carrying.

## The floor, and why it is arithmetic rather than opinion

Most pages on this question refuse to name a bottom. Here is one, because it follows from the hourly rates above rather than from anyone's positioning.

**The floor for one channel handled properly by one competent person is about $1,500 a month, or Rs 15,000 to Rs 20,000 in India.** Below that, the arithmetic stops working.

Run it from the seller's side. At Rs 500 an hour, the floor for anyone competent, Rs 8,000 buys sixteen hours. Take out the monthly report, the two calls, and the tool subscriptions the agency is paying out of that same amount. You are left with perhaps six to eight hours of real work. Split six hours across four channels and you have ninety minutes per channel per month. Ninety minutes is enough to schedule some posts and change a bid. It is not enough to read a search terms report, form a view and act on it.

So the Rs 8,000 four-channel offer is not usually a scam. The person is generally real and generally working. What is fictional is the scope, and it was fictional before anyone signed. The dishonesty sits in the deliverables list, not in the effort.

The reasonable version of a small budget is one channel, done properly, by one person who answers your messages. Rs 15,000 spent that way beats Rs 15,000 spread across four channels every time, and it is not close. The same logic applies to [splitting a small budget across channels](/blog/how-to-split-a-marketing-budget-india/) and to [working out what you should be spending in the first place](/blog/digital-marketing-budget-small-business-india/).

## Before you sign anything

Three questions, and the answers matter more than the price:

1. How many hours a month, and what is the blended rate that implies?
2. Who specifically does the work, what is their title, and how many other accounts do they have?
3. What is deliberately not included?

A quote that survives all three is a real quote. One that does not is a package, and a package is a price with a story attached.

I have written separately about [the structural questions to ask before signing](/blog/how-to-choose-a-digital-marketing-agency/), particularly around who owns your ad accounts when the relationship ends, which is worth reading before you commit to anyone at any price. If you are weighing a named agency against an independent, [how to choose a digital marketing consultant](/blog/how-to-choose-a-digital-marketing-consultant/) covers the diagnostic questions that separate operators from people selling retainers.

The short version of all of this: stop comparing the monthly numbers against each other and start converting them into hours of a named person's attention. Once you do that, the tenfold spread in quotes you have been staring at usually resolves into something that makes sense.
