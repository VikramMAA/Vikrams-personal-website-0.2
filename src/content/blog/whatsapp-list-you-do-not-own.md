If your business runs on the WhatsApp Business API, everything a customer said to you before the 26th of October is gone.

Not archived somewhere you have not looked. Not held by Meta on your behalf, waiting for you to ask. Gone, unless you were storing it yourself as it arrived.

And Meta's documentation is completely open about why, which is the part nobody seems to have read.

## What the documentation actually says

From [Meta's data privacy and security page for the WhatsApp Business Platform](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/data-privacy-and-security), under stored and collected data:

> "Message Data. Messages have a maximum retention period of 30 days in order to provide the base features and functionality of the Cloud API service; for example, retransmissions."

And immediately after it:

> "User Data. Cloud API uses identifiers as sources or destinations of individual messages; as such, Cloud API deletes them within 30 days of the last status update (sent, delivered, read) of a message, unless otherwise directed."

The same page says that Meta, in providing the Cloud API, "acts as a data processor/service provider on behalf of the business", that the API "will only use the messages it processes on behalf of and at the instruction of the business", and that "businesses can use messages they receive for their own marketing purposes".

Read that as a set and the picture changes.

Meta is not hoarding your customer relationships. It is handing them to you in real time, through your webhook, and then forgetting them on a thirty day timer.

## Which means the usual worry is the wrong worry

The platform dependency conversation is almost always about eviction. The rules change. The pricing changes. The account gets restricted and nobody answers your appeal. Those risks are real, they have already materialised twice, and I have written about [the pricing change and the three things to fix](/blog/whatsapp-marketing-india/) and about [where the lead actually gets recorded](/blog/track-whatsapp-leads-from-website/), so I am not going to run either of those again here.

Here is the thing about eviction, though. Eviction is survivable if you have the records.

What is not survivable is finding out during the eviction that you have no records, because you assumed the channel was the record.

If you are on the Business app rather than the API, your position is different and not better. The history does exist. It exists on a handset, in a form you cannot query, search across, or hand to a new employee, belonging in practice to whoever is holding the phone. That is a single point of failure with legs.

## The asset is three things and only one of them lives in WhatsApp

**The phone numbers.** You collected these. They are yours, they export, and this is the part everybody thinks the argument is about. It is the easy part.

**The consent.** Also yours to keep, and there is now a legal reason to keep it properly rather than a tidiness reason. I went through the Indian data protection timeline and what to record in [lead magnet ideas for B2B in India](/blog/lead-magnet-ideas-b2b-india/) and will not repeat the dates here.

**The context.** What this customer asked for. What you quoted. What they chose instead, and why. What went wrong in March and how you fixed it. Which size they take. Who in their office actually places the order. In their words, not your categories.

That third thing is where almost all the value sits, it exists only inside the conversation, and it is the one the platform has told you in writing that it does not keep.

You can port a phone number in an afternoon. You cannot port a relationship you never wrote down.

## Price the exposure instead of agonising over it

I want to be clear that I am not telling you to leave. You should not leave. It works, your customers are already there, nobody in India opens an email from a hardware supplier, and no alternative channel reaches them as reliably. Moralising about platform dependency is cheap and it has never helped anybody.

The useful question is narrower and almost nobody asks it.

**How expensive is the next rule change allowed to be?**

Three figures, and you can estimate all of them this week. What share of your revenue arrives through or completes on WhatsApp. How many of those customers you could reach another way inside seven days, by a route you have actually used. And how long it would take you to stand up a second channel from nothing, honestly, including the week nobody replies.

Most owners have never worked those out, which means they are carrying an unpriced exposure. Unpriced is not the same as large. It might turn out to be small and the right answer might be to do nothing. But you do not currently know which, and that is a strange position to be in about the thing your business runs on.

## Four things to hold outside the platform

None of this requires leaving, or new software, or a project.

**The numbers, with the source and the date you got them.** A spreadsheet is fine. Where it came from matters as much as the number.

**The consent record.** What they agreed to receive, when, and how you asked.

**A three line summary of every conversation that mattered.** Not a transcript. What they wanted, what they got, what to remember next time. Written by whoever had the conversation, on the day, into something you own. This is the discipline that feels like overhead for four months and then saves a relationship when the person who handled it leaves.

**One tested alternative route to your best fifty customers.** Tested, not theoretical. Send them something genuinely useful by that route once, now, while you do not need it, so you know whether it works and so they recognise it when you do.

That last one is the whole insurance policy and it costs an afternoon.

## Where I come out

Meta is not the villain in this story, and the documentation is unusually plain about it. It describes itself as a processor, it deletes the data on a timer, and it explicitly tells businesses to use their own messages for their own purposes.

That sentence is written as a permission. Read it as a warning.

The businesses that will be in trouble are not the ones that get evicted from WhatsApp. They are the ones that spent five years treating a transport channel as a filing cabinet, and found out in a single afternoon that it was never one.
