Your website quote contains two kinds of number.

One kind is published on a vendor's website and you can verify it before lunch. The other is labour, and there is no market price for it, which is why three quotes for the same brief can sit anywhere from twenty thousand rupees to four lakh.

Nobody separates those two for you, so let's do it in that order.

## The part you can check yourself

I went and looked this morning rather than quoting from memory, because these numbers move.

**Hosting.** On [Hostinger's India site](https://www.hostinger.in/web-hosting) today, the entry shared hosting plan shows at Rs 69 a month. Read the line underneath it: "Today you pay Rs 3,312 for 48 months. Renews at Rs 289." So the advertised price is a four-year prepayment, and the actual ongoing rate is roughly four times the headline. The next plan up shows Rs 139 a month and renews at Rs 449.

That renewal gap is the single most common surprise in small business web costs, and it is printed on the page.

**Domain.** The same site lists a domain from Rs 399. First-year domain pricing is almost always promotional, so check what the renewal costs before you pick the extension.

**If you are selling online**, you may not need a build at all. [Shopify's India pricing](https://www.shopify.com/in/pricing) today is Rs 1,499 a month on Basic, Rs 5,599 on Grow and Rs 22,680 on Advanced. Basic works out around eighteen thousand rupees a year for a hosted store that somebody else keeps running and secure.

**Payments.** Your gateway takes a percentage of every transaction. The rate is published on the provider's site and it belongs in your arithmetic from day one, not in a surprise in month three. I am not quoting a figure here because the page I checked served me the wrong country's rate card, which tells you something about checking it yourself on your own account.

So: somewhere between a few thousand rupees a year for a brochure site and around twenty thousand for a hosted store. That is the floor, it is verifiable, and it is not what your quote is about.

## What actually moves the labour number

Five decisions, and they explain almost the whole spread. If a quote does not state where it sits on each of these, it is not a quote, it is a number.

**How many pages are actually designed.** A five-page site can mean five designed pages or one template filled in five times. Both are legitimate. They cost very different amounts and the second is often the right answer for a small business.

**Who writes the copy.** This is the biggest hidden variable in the entire market. "Content to be provided by client" moves real work and real cost off the quote and onto you, and if you are honest about whether you will do it, you already know how that ends.

**Whether existing content is migrated or retyped.** Moving forty pages from an old site is mechanical work somebody has to do. It is frequently not mentioned until after signing.

**What happens to an enquiry.** Where the form lands, whether it reaches WhatsApp, whether anybody gets a notification, whether you can tell which page produced it. A form that emails an address nobody checks is a website that does not work, and it passes every test an agency runs before handover.

**Integrations.** Payments, a booking calendar, Shiprocket, a CRM, Justdial or IndiaMART feeds, GST-compliant invoicing. Each one is a day or two that is either in the price or is not.

For an anchor, and I am giving you this as a range from experience rather than a published figure: in Bengaluru I see a template-based five-page site with client-supplied copy quoted in the low tens of thousands, and the same brief with original design and written copy at several times that. Both numbers can be fair. On why the quotes for marketing work vary even more wildly than this, I went through the mechanics in [what digital marketing actually costs in India](/blog/digital-marketing-consultant-cost-india/).

## Three traps worth checking in any quote

**The site built on a page builder the agency controls.** Ask what the site is built on and whether the licence is in your name. If the design lives inside a builder on the agency's account, you are renting your website and you will discover it the month you want to leave.

**The copy that never gets written.** A quote that leaves content to you and does not say so prominently will produce a beautiful site with lorem ipsum in two sections and a launch date that slips by four months. If you know you will not write it, pay somebody to, and put it in this quote rather than next year's.

**The annual maintenance charge that re-sells the same work.** Maintenance is real: updates, backups, security patches, breakages. Ask exactly what the AMC covers, how many hours of changes it includes, and what a change outside it costs. An AMC that covers "updates and support" and nothing specific is a subscription to being told your request is out of scope.

## The questions to ask before you sign

Five questions, and what a good answer sounds like.

*Whose name is the domain registered in?* Mine, with the login details handed over. Anything else is a hostage situation waiting to happen.

*Whose account is the hosting on?* Mine, ideally, or at least an account I can be moved to without rebuilding.

*What is the site built on?* A named platform you can look up, with no licence tied to the agency.

*Who writes the words, and is that in this price?* A direct answer either way. "We'll work with you on that" is a no.

*What happens on day one after launch if something breaks?* A named person, a response time, and whether it costs extra.

Ask the first two in writing. They cost nothing to answer and the answer tells you most of what you need to know about the rest.

## Who owns what afterwards

The thing that makes a cheap website expensive is almost never the build price. It is that two years later you want to change something and cannot, because the domain is in somebody else's account, the hosting is on their reseller plan, the theme licence is theirs, and nobody can find the original files.

Get the domain, the hosting login and the admin login in your name on the day of launch. Not promised, held. Everything else is recoverable.

## Before lunch

Take the quote in front of you and mark each of the five labour decisions above as stated or unstated. Then send two sentences asking whose name the domain and hosting will be in.

If more than two of the five are unstated, you do not have a quote you can compare against another one, and asking for the detail is not rude. It is the only way the second quote means anything.
