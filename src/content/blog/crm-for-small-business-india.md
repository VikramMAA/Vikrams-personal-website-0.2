Open WhatsApp and look at the top of your chat list.

That is the person who messaged you most recently. Which means, almost by definition, it is the person who needs chasing least. They are already talking to you.

The lead who went quiet eleven days ago, the one who asked for a quote and then stopped replying, is somewhere down the list below a plumber, a school group and your cousin.

That is not a discipline problem. It is the sort order, and you cannot fix a sort order by trying harder.

So, the answer. You probably do not need CRM software yet. What you need is one list that sorts by when you next have to act, which is the single thing WhatsApp cannot give you at any level of organisation. Four columns and a date does it. Software earns its money at two specific thresholds, and I will name them.

## Why WhatsApp loses leads

Every tool the app gives you sorts by the past rather than the future.

Archiving is a judgement about a conversation that is over. Starring produces a pile with no order inside it, so after three weeks you have forty stars and no idea which one is due. Labels on a Business account are better, and they still tell you what kind of lead somebody is rather than when you promised to come back to them.

The field that would fix it does not exist. There is nowhere in WhatsApp to write "call on the 24th", and no view that shows you everything due today.

**A pipeline is not a list of people. It is a list of dates.**

That is why the owner who is genuinely hard working, who answers every message within minutes, still loses a third of their quotes to silence. The app rewards responsiveness and has no opinion at all about persistence.

## The personal number problem, which is a business risk

Before the system, one thing that is more urgent than any of this, and almost nobody treats it as a risk.

If your sales conversations happen on somebody's personal number, then the record of what your business promised its customers lives on a phone your business does not own.

Work through what that means. When that person leaves, the relationships leave with them, and so does the only evidence of what was agreed about price, timeline and scope. Nobody else can cover their leads during leave, because nobody else can see the thread. And the backup, if there is one, is in a personal Google account you have no claim on.

I have seen this cost real money in small Indian companies and it is entirely preventable. Put sales on a number the business controls, on a WhatsApp Business account, with the SIM in the company's name and the recovery email and two-factor details held by the owner. Do that before you optimise anything else.

## The four columns

Here is the whole system, and it is a spreadsheet.

Name and number. What they asked for, in their own words rather than your category for it. Where they came from. Next action, and the date it is due.

That is it. Sort by the date column. Filter to today.

Two operating rules make it work and nothing else does.

The sheet is updated at the end of the conversation, not at the end of the week. Thirty seconds while you still remember, because a batch of updates on Saturday is a memory test you will fail.

And if it is not in the sheet it does not exist. No parallel system, no "I will remember this one", no leads living only in your head because they felt important. The moment there are two systems there is no system.

Keep "what they asked for" in their words, because that is the part that lets you write a follow up worth reading three weeks later. "Needs it before his daughter's wedding in December" is useful. "Hot lead" is not.

## The cadence for leads that went quiet

This is a different problem from answering fast, which I have written about separately as [the decay in the first hour](/blog/b2b-lead-follow-up-speed/). That post is about minute one. This is about day eleven, and it is where most of the recoverable money sits.

A cadence that works for most Indian service businesses: day two, day five, day twelve, day thirty, then once a quarter.

The spacing matters less than the rule about content. Every touch has to carry something. "Just following up" is a request for attention with nothing offered in return, and after the second one it reads as pestering.

Three things qualify as something. A piece of news that genuinely affects their decision. A specific answer to a question they actually raised, even if you answered it before and are now answering it better. Or a deadline that is theirs rather than yours, like a price change or a slot that will go.

Then the move most people never make. After the fourth attempt, send the message that asks permission to close the file. Something like: I do not want to keep bothering you, so shall I close this off and you can come back whenever suits?

That gets a reply more often than any follow up I have seen, because it asks for a decision rather than for attention, and a decision is easier to give. Some of those replies are a no, which is also useful, because a no removes a row from a sheet that was quietly making you feel behind.

## When software actually earns the money

Two thresholds, and both of them are about memory rather than features.

The first: more than one person needs to see the same conversation. The moment two people are selling, a spreadsheet of threads on two phones stops being a system.

The second: you can no longer reconstruct what was said three weeks ago. Not who the lead is, what was promised. When your memory stops being a reliable database, you need an actual one.

Until one of those is true, software is overhead.

The prices are not the obstacle, which is worth saying because people assume they are. Bigin's own India pricing page puts its Express edition at around Rs 400 per user per month billed annually, before GST, with a free single user tier. Zoho CRM Standard sits closer to Rs 800 on the same basis. Third party comparison sites quote higher figures than the vendor does, so check the vendor's page.

The real cost is twenty minutes a day of somebody entering things. That is why most CRMs in small companies are abandoned by month three, and it is not a licensing decision, it is a staffing one. On choosing between platforms once you are past the threshold, the question that actually settles it is [who will be in the settings screen on a Tuesday in March](/blog/hubspot-vs-salesforce-small-business/).

## Before lunch

Forty minutes, in order.

Check whose number your sales conversations are on. If it is a personal one, start the process of moving to a business number today, because everything else is less urgent than this.

Then open a sheet, make the four columns, and go through your WhatsApp chat list from the top. Every conversation that is a live enquiry gets a row and a next action date, including the ones you have been avoiding.

Then sort by date and look at how many are already overdue. That number is your actual answer to whether you have a system.
