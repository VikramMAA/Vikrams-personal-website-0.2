# Estonia keyword strategy — vikramhere.com

Built 21 August 2026. 20 clusters: 4 county-level, 16 national. Written in
English for Estonian companies, never in Estonian.

## The one fact that shapes everything

Estonia has about 1.3 million people. Domestic search volume for marketing
services is tiny, and no amount of keyword work changes that.

The companies worth writing for **sell outward** — into the Nordics, the wider
EU, and the US. So this plan is only lightly about ranking for Estonian demand,
and mostly about being the useful English-language source for Estonian founders
solving an export problem. That is a far more winnable position than competing
for `turundusagentuur Tallinn`.

## Counties: four, not ten

The instruction was top 10 counties. Estonia has 15, and the data does not
support 10. Registered companies by county, from the Estonian Business Register:

| County | Seat | Registered companies | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Harju | Tallinn | 202,347 | Effectively the national market |
| Tartu | Tartu | 36,480 | University and deep tech cluster |
| Pärnu | Pärnu | 18,238 | Seasonal tourism and hospitality |
| Ida-Viru | Narva | 15,299 | Industry in transition, Russian speaking |

Harju alone takes roughly 69% of new company registrations. The remaining
eleven counties (Lääne-Viru, Viljandi, Rapla, Võru, Järva, Valga, Jõgeva,
Saare, Hiiu, Põlva, Lääne) are **parked permanently**, not queued for a later
tier. Several have fewer registered businesses than a single Tallinn office
block, and a page targeting them would have no searcher behind it.

This is a case where following the brief literally would have produced eleven
pages nobody will ever search for. The four that remain are real.

Each county cluster carries `local_specifics` so the posts differ genuinely:
Tartu's research-led pre-revenue companies have a different marketing problem
from Pärnu's June-to-August seasonality, and Ida-Viru's Russian-speaking market
changes the language strategy outright.

## Where the winnable demand actually is

The national layer is the substance of this plan, and it scores higher than the
geo layer because the competition is thin and the specificity is high.

**The strongest cluster is payments.** Estonians expect to pay by bank link
through Swedbank, SEB or LHV rather than entering a card number, and Stripe
alone does not offer Estonian bank links. Montonio and Maksekeskus do. A shop
running Stripe-only is losing checkout conversions for a reason its owner
probably cannot see. Almost nobody writing in English covers this properly.

Others in the same vein, all verified against provider and government sources:

- Omniva, SmartPost and DPD parcel lockers as the assumed delivery method
- e-Residency companies and the trust gap they hit with cautious EU buyers
- Hreflang for Estonian/English bilingual sites
- Google Ads below the conversion volume smart bidding needs to learn
- 24% VAT since July 2025, which makes most existing content stale
- 0% tax on retained profits versus 22% on distribution, which genuinely changes
  how growth spend should be funded
- EU data residency as a live sales argument rather than a compliance cost

## Fit with Vikram specifically

The `selling saas to us customers from europe` and `b2b lead generation nordics`
clusters are where he has an unfair advantage: he already works across
geographies, so the export-corridor material is first-hand rather than
researched. Those score highest on fit.

The weakest fit is anything requiring Estonian-language execution. The plan
avoids it entirely rather than faking it, and VOICE.md forbids Estonian phrases
beyond place names for the same reason.

## Confidence and gaps

**Medium.** What is missing:

- No Search Console data and no paid volume source for this market.
- SERP checks were done on the English-language commercial terms only. Terms
  where Estonian-language content dominates were not assessed in depth, because
  competing there is out of scope by design.
- Company-count data is a proxy for search demand, not a measurement of it. It
  is a good proxy for ranking counties against each other and a poor one for
  estimating absolute traffic.

The realistic expectation: Estonia is a low-volume, high-specificity market.
It will never produce the traffic India or the US can. It is worth doing because
the competition for genuinely useful English content is close to absent, and
because a few good export-focused clients pay in euro.
